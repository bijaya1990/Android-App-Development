## How to read this book

The book does three jobs at once. It is a **research guide** to what traditional sources say about Yama’s realm, a **series bible** for an original comedy vlog, and a **prompt library** for AI image and video tools. Keeping those three jobs apart is the most important rule in the book, so every claim carries one of three labels:

| Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {SRC} | A claim supported by a cited traditional source or its translation. A citation sits beside it. | The Vaitarani is described as a hundred yojanas wide (Vedic People, Preta Khaṇḍa ch. 47, v. 3). |
| {INT} | A reasonable reading of a source passage, offered as a reading and not as the text itself. | Saumya, the first city on the path, works well as a “gentle” location because the text says souls there feel some relief. |
| {FIC} | Invented for Saapwaa’s vlog: places, people, shops, rules, conversations and incidents. | Kulhad Didi’s tea stall and its tea that is never cold. |
| {UNC} | A detail that could not be verified in this batch. It is kept out of the factual guide until checked. | Chapter numbering on sacred-texts.com (the site refused access during research). |

<div class="box" markdown="1">
**The promise to readers and viewers.** No toilet, kitchen, food stall, queue, tea, workshop, office window or local rule in this book is claimed to come from the Garuda Purana. Those are Saapwaa’s fictional neighbourhood. When the series visits a place that the texts do describe, the scene says so and cites it.
</div>

## Tone and respect

The comedy comes from Saapwaa: a modern, slightly vain, curious vlogger living in an invented residential corner of Narak and reacting to its everyday inconveniences. It does not come from mocking Yama, Chitragupta, deities, priests, rituals, devotees or the moral teaching of the texts. Scenes never show torment for laughs, and the punishments described in the sources are summarised soberly in the research guide and never staged as gags.

## A note on AI tools

Every scene has a standalone image prompt and a standalone video prompt under 900 characters. The prompts are written to reduce common failure causes (crowds, chained actions, written text, changing locations), but no prompt can guarantee a clean result. Output depends on the generator, its model version and its settings. Part XI of the finished book covers troubleshooting; the short version is: simplify, split the shot, or generate silently and add the voice in the edit.
