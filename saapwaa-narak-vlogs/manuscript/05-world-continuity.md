# 4 · The Narak World and Continuity Guide

*Where Saapwaa lives, what is real in the sources and what is invented, and the ledger that keeps 25 episodes consistent.*

## The premise

{FIC} Saapwaa lives in **Dhuan Tola** (“Smoke Quarter”), an invented settlement of cave homes beside the road the sources describe — the way a village sits beside a highway. Residents here live ordinary, inconvenient lives: they fetch water, refill lamps, drink tea, borrow things and forget to return them. Nobody is tormented here; the series never shows torment.

{INT} The sources describe the road to Yama and its sixteen cities as stations on a journey, not as neighbourhoods with daily life (Chapter 2). Treating a settlement as existing beside that road is the series’ own invention and is always labelled as such.

**Why is Saapwaa here?** Never explained. “वो लंबी कहानी है।”

## The look of Dhuan Tola

<div class="box lock" markdown="1">
**Environment lock (paste into prompts set in the neighbourhood):**
`Dhuan Tola, a rocky cave neighbourhood in Narak: basalt-grey and rust-red rock, warm oil-lamp and torch light, smoky maroon sky with no sun, faint drifting embers.`
</div>

- **Light:** warm amber from clay oil lamps and wall torches. Fire is small and controlled; no infernos.
- **Sky:** a low, smoky maroon ceiling. No sun, moon or stars.
- **Rock:** grey basalt with rust-red streaks, smoothed by use; steps cut into slopes.
- **People:** ordinary cartoon adults in faded cotton clothes, calm and a bit tired. No wounds, chains or gore.
- **Sound bed for the edit:** distant wind, crackling lamps, a tea kettle, occasional crow calls.

### Places that are never staged

Yama’s court, Chitragupta’s hall, the southern gate as a place of judgement, any naraka named in the sources, the silk-cotton tree, and anyone being punished. They may be *mentioned* by a guide with a citation; they are not shown.

## Fictional rules of daily life

All {FIC}. These give episodes something to push against.

1. **Lamps first.** Every home refills its lamp at the start of the “day”, signalled by Lalten Lal ringing a brass bell.
2. **All water is warm.** “Cold” means “not boiling.”
3. **Borrowing is normal; returning is a virtue.** Most people manage the first part.
4. **Cowrie shells (कौड़ी) are money.** Tea costs two.
5. **The Rest Bench has no time limit.** That is the problem.
6. **Shade is shared.** You may join anyone’s shadow if you ask first.

{SRC}-inspired running gag: S3 (ch. 3, v. 79) says a day in naraka equals one hundred mortal years. Saapwaa does this sum about his followers on Earth in Episode 6. The joke is about time and his ego; it makes no claim about his own judgement or fate.
