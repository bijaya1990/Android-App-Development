# 2 · Research and Source Plan

*How the factual side of the book is researched, checked and cited — plus the facts verified for Batch 1.*

## Research rules used in this book

1. **Read the passage before using the claim.** Nothing enters the factual guide from memory or from a secondary summary alone.
2. **Record the location precisely**: source, chapter, verse (śloka) range where the edition gives one, and the page of the PDF where the edition is a PDF.
3. **Compare editions.** Chapter numbers and figures differ between editions; differences are logged, not smoothed over.
4. **Summarise in original words.** Modern translations are copyrighted. The book paraphrases and quotes only short phrases.
5. **Never invent** a quotation, verse number or reference. If a detail cannot be checked it is labelled {UNC} or left out.
6. **Keep a source log** (below) and carry it forward in every batch.

## Verified facts for the series (Batch 1)

Each fact below was checked in the passage cited. These are the facts that scenes may use.

### The road to Yama

- {SRC} Excluding the Vaitarani, the way to Yama’s domain measures **eighty-six thousand yojanas**, and the soul in its new body covers **247 yojanas in a day and night** (S4, ch. 1, closing section, ślokas 47–60, PDF p. 8). S4 glosses a yojana as roughly eight miles.
- {SRC} The road passes through **sixteen cities**: Saumya, Sauripura, Nagendra Bhavana, Gandharva, Shailagama, Krauncha, Kroora, Vichitra Bhavana, Bahwapada, Duhkhada, Nanakrandana, Sutapta, Raudra, Payovarsha, Sheetadhya and Bahubheeti (S4, PDF p. 8; spellings as in S4).
- {SRC} The path is described as dark and rough, with **no shade, no food and no water**, heat like “ten suns,” or else freezing wind; thorns; a forest of **sword-like leaves**; pits, hot sand, smoke and showers of coals and stones (S4, ch. 2 “Yama’s Abode”, ślokas 2–32, PDF p. 9).
- {SRC} Yama’s messengers are described as fierce and foul-smelling, carrying clubs and sticks (S3, ch. 2, vv. 50–52).
- {SRC} **Saumya**, the first city, is described as beautiful; there is a fig tree and the river **Pushpabhadra**, and the travellers rest a little (S4, ch. 2, ślokas 42–47, PDF p. 11).
- {SRC} The kinkaras ask the soul what **provisions** it carries, on a road where “nothing would be sold and nothing could be bought” (S4, ch. 2, ślokas 48–49, PDF p. 11). *This line is the reason the series never puts a shop on the sacred road itself: every stall in the show is in the invented neighbourhood.*
- {SRC} **Sheetadhya** is named for extreme cold, said to be a hundred times harder to bear than Himalayan cold (S4, ch. 2, ślokas 70–81, PDF p. 12).

### The Vaitarani

- {SRC} The Vaitarani is described as a mighty river at the threshold of Yama’s city, **a hundred yojanas wide**, impassable and foul-smelling (S3, ch. 47, vv. 2–3). S4 says it “runs for a hundred yojanas” (S4, ch. 2, PDF p. 9).
- {SRC} Those who made **gifts during life** cross it easily; others sink (S3, ch. 47, v. 8).
- {SRC} At the city of Vichitra (Chitrabhavana), ruled by King Vichitra (described as Yama’s brother), **fishermen** offer to ferry the soul across if it has merit. They explain the name: the river can be crossed only by *vitaraṇa*, **giving** (S4, ch. 2, ślokas 57–69, PDF p. 12).
- {SRC} The rite of giving a cow (the Vaitarani gift) is described with its offerings, and is said to ease the crossing (S3, ch. 47, vv. 25–38; S4, ch. 8, ślokas 77–90, PDF p. 33).

### Yama’s city and court

- {SRC} Yama’s city has **four gates**; the virtuous enter by the east, west and north, and sinners by the south (S4, ch. 4, ślokas 1–3, PDF p. 17; also ch. 3, PDF p. 13).
- {SRC} At the gate stands **Dharmadhvaja**, who reports to **Chitragupta**; the **Śravaṇas** and their wives, the Śravaṇīs, report deeds truthfully (S4, ch. 3, ślokas 3–24, PDF p. 14).
- {SRC} The city of Yama is described as splendid: four-cornered, with four gateways and high ramparts, hundreds of decorated streets, gardens and a pleasant climate for the meritorious who live there; Chitragupta keeps the record of all deeds (S4, ch. 14 “The Court of the Deity of Death”, ślokas 1–30, PDF pp. 50–51).

### Narakas and time

- {SRC} S4 lists twenty-one principal narakas, including Tamisra, Raurava, Andha Tamisra and Kumbhipaka (S4, ch. 3, ślokas 60–64, PDF p. 16).
- {SRC} S3 names a very cold hell, **Atiśīta** (S3, ch. 3, v. 17), and says **a day in hell equals one hundred mortal years** (S3, ch. 3, v. 79).

## Where the sources differ

| Topic | S4 says | S3 says | How the book handles it |

| Vaitarani size | “Runs for a hundred yojanas” | “A hundred yojanas wide” (ch. 47, v. 3) | Both say a hundred yojanas; the book says “a hundred yojanas” without choosing length or width. |

| The cold place | Sheetadhya, the fifteenth **city** on the road | Atiśīta, a **hell** | These are different things in different contexts. The series uses Sheetadhya (a city) for its cold-trip episode and never merges the two. |
| Ch. 2 speaker | Vishnu (Sri Hari) to Garuda | Verse text presents the speaker as Krishna | Not relevant to scenes; noted for Batch 2. |

## What the sources do not describe

{INT} The texts describe a road, a river, cities passed through, Yama’s city and court, and places of consequence. They do not describe a daily residential life: no kitchens, tea, toilets, markets, rooms, repair shops, queues for water or office windows for ordinary residents. That gap is deliberate territory for the series. Everything Saapwaa does at home and in his neighbourhood is labelled {FIC}.

## Respect rules drawn from the research

1. Never show torment, punishment or suffering as a gag. The sources describe them; the series does not stage them.
2. Never caricature Yama, Chitragupta, Dharmadhvaja, the Śravaṇas, deities, Brahmins, cows or rituals. When they are mentioned, they are mentioned with the sources’ own dignity.
3. The giving theme of the Vaitarani is the one moral idea the series returns to, and it is always played gently and against Saapwaa himself (he is stingy with snacks).
4. No scene claims a fictional rule is scriptural. When a fictional resident states a “rule”, the scene status says {FIC}.
